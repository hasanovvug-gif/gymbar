# The six movement patterns

Audit by pattern, not by muscle name. A muscle can look "covered" while the movement that trains
half of it is absent — squats extend the knee and never flex it, so a leg day of squats plus leg
extensions trains the front of the thigh twice and the back of it zero times.

Map every exercise in the program onto the table. Any pattern with an empty row is a finding.

| # | Pattern | Example movements | What it covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Horizontal push | bench press, incline press, push-up, cable chest press | chest, front delt, triceps |
| 2 | Vertical push | overhead press, dumbbell shoulder press | front and side delt, triceps |
| 3 | Horizontal pull | barbell row, seated cable row, inverted row | mid back, rear delt, biceps |
| 4 | Vertical pull | pull-up, lat pulldown | lats, biceps |
| 5 | Knee-dominant | squat, lunge, split squat, leg press, leg extension | quadriceps, glutes |
| 6 | Hip-dominant | deadlift, Romanian deadlift, hip thrust, back extension | hamstrings, glutes, spinal erectors |

Plus one that is not a "pattern" but is missed just as often:

| 6b | Knee flexion | leg curl in any form | hamstrings, specifically the short head of biceps femoris |

## Why knee flexion needs its own line

The Romanian deadlift trains the hamstrings under stretch and is excellent — but the short head of
biceps femoris does not cross the hip joint. No amount of hinging reaches it. It is trained by
bending the knee against resistance and by nothing else.

This is the most common hole in home and outdoor setups, because the leg curl machine is the one
piece nobody replicates. It is also the easiest to fix cheaply — see below.

## Building each pattern from limited equipment

**Barbell + plates + rack**
Covers 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 completely. Bench, overhead press, row, squat, deadlift, Romanian deadlift.
Missing: vertical pull (needs a bar to hang from) and knee flexion.

**Dumbbells only**
Covers 1, 2, 3 (one-arm row), 5 (goblet squat, lunge, split squat), 6 (Romanian deadlift, hip
thrust). Missing: vertical pull, knee flexion. Load ceiling arrives early on squats and hinges —
switch to single-leg variants rather than chasing heavier dumbbells.

**Cable stack / functional trainer**
Covers 1 (cable press), 3 (seated row), 4 (pulldown), and 6 partially (pull-through, cable
hinge). With an **ankle strap** it also covers knee flexion — the cheapest fix for the most common
hole. Ask whether they have one before declaring knee flexion impossible.

**Bodyweight only**
Covers 1 (push-up, dip), 4 (pull-up if a bar exists), 5 (squat, lunge, pistol progression),
6 (hip thrust, single-leg RDL, back extension on a bench, Nordic curl if the feet can be anchored
securely). Missing: loaded vertical push and loaded horizontal pull without a bar to row under.

**Back-extension bench**
Covers hip extension partially. It trains glutes and the two-joint hamstrings when the movement is
driven by the hips. It does **not** train knee flexion and does not replace a leg curl.

## Nordic curl — the honest caveat

Nordic curls do train knee flexion, but they require the feet to be anchored by something that
will not move under sudden load. A partner, a loaded barbell, or a purpose-built anchor. Do not
suggest wedging feet under an unloaded bar, a sofa, or a fence rail. If nothing reliable exists,
say knee flexion cannot be built rather than proposing an improvised anchor.

## Movements worth flagging as low value

Not banned, just usually replaceable when the program is already full:

- **Upright row** — overlaps front and side delt work that presses and lateral raises already
  cover, and the high-elbow position bothers some shoulders. If it stays, it should be because
  the person likes it, not because it fills a gap.
- **A fourth pressing variation** in the same session — by then the chest is fatigued and the sets
  add cost without much stimulus.
- **Loaded side bends** — the obliques are already working in every carry, squat and standing
  press in the program.
