# Training alone

Ask one question before writing any barbell work into the program:

> Do you have a rack, and does that rack have safety pins or arms set at chest height?

The answer changes what belongs in the program. Do not assume — "I have a rack" often means
uprights to lift the bar off, with nothing to catch it.

## With safety pins

Barbell bench, incline and back squat stay in. Set the pins so a failed rep lands on them rather
than on the lifter, and keep RIR 2–3 on those lifts — near-failure barbell work alone is still a
bad trade even with pins.

## Without pins, training alone

Two lifts are the actual risk in a typical program. Everything else fails safely.

**Barbell bench press.** A failed rep leaves the bar on the chest or throat with no way out.
Replace with:
- dumbbell press — dump the dumbbells sideways and nothing happens
- cable or machine chest press
- weighted push-up or deficit push-up
- barbell bench with clips **off**, so plates can be tipped off one side — a last resort, not a
  plan

**Back squat.** A failed rep leaves the bar on the back with no controlled exit. Replace with:
- Bulgarian split squat or reverse lunge with dumbbells — heavy stimulus, small absolute load
- goblet squat
- leg press or cable squat if available
- front squat only for someone who can reliably dump it forward

## Other lifts, alone

- **Deadlift** — safe alone: a failed rep is a rep that doesn't leave the floor. Form under
  fatigue is the concern, not entrapment. Stop the set when the back starts rounding.
- **Overhead press** — ask where the bar comes from. Off a rack: fine. Cleaned from the floor
  every set: the clean becomes the limiting factor and fatigues them before the press does.
  Without a rack, do not push to failure; dumbbells overhead are the safer default.
- **Romanian deadlift** — safe. The bar is set down, not caught.
- **Dips and pull-ups** — safe; the failure mode is stepping off.

## Outdoors, specifically

- Level, dry, non-slip surface. A barbell on soft ground tips.
- No pressing or squatting on wet surfaces.
- Cold muscles need a longer warm-up than the same session indoors.
- Check that the ground can take a dropped loaded bar before planning to drop one.

## Always, regardless of setup

- No one-rep-max testing alone. Ever.
- No barbell press or squat to technical failure alone.
- Pain is not something to push through, and it is not something a program can fix — that belongs
  to a medical professional.
