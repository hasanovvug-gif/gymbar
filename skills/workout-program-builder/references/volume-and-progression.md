# Volume, reps and progression

## Rep ranges by goal

Pick the row that matches the stated goal. Do not infer the goal from bodyweight.

| Goal | Compounds | Machines / cables | Isolation | Small muscles, calves, delts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Muscle size | 6–10 or 8–12 | 8–15 | 10–15 | 12–20 |
| Strength | 3–6 | 6–10 | 8–12 | 10–15 |
| Fat loss (keeping muscle) | 6–12 | 8–15 | 10–15 | 12–20 |
| General health | 8–12 | 10–15 | 10–15 | 12–20 |

A frequent real-world finding: someone training for size is working at 12–15+ reps on every lift.
That is not wrong in itself — high reps taken close to failure grow muscle much like heavy ones —
but in practice it usually means the load is well short of what they could handle, and the sets
end far from failure. Moving compounds to 8–12 fixes it without any change in effort.

## Weekly hard sets

Around **10 hard sets per muscle group per week**, each group trained **at least twice**, is the
current mainstream orientation point (ACSM 2026 resistance-training guidance). More can work; the
returns flatten and recovery cost rises.

Use it as a compass, not a target. **Asymmetry between groups is the finding that matters** — four
shoulder exercises against zero for the posterior chain is a defect regardless of the totals.

A "hard set" means taken near failure. Warm-ups don't count.

## Proximity to failure — RIR

RIR = reps in reserve, how many more you could have done.

- Most working sets: **RIR 1–3**.
- Barbell bench and squat without a spotter: **RIR 2–3 minimum**, never to failure.
- Isolation on cables and machines, where failure is harmless: **RIR 0–2** is fine.

Training to absolute failure on everything is not required for growth and costs disproportionate
recovery. Getting close matters; arriving does not.

## Double progression — the rule that replaces guessing

The single most useful thing to hand someone. It removes every "should I add weight today?"
decision.

1. Pick a rep range, e.g. 3 × 8–12.
2. Choose a load where all sets are clean at RIR 2–3.
3. Keep the load and **add reps** session to session: `10/9/8 → 11/10/9 → 12/11/10 → 12/12/12`.
4. When you hit the top of the range on **all sets, two sessions in a row**, add weight.
5. Reps drop back toward the bottom of the range. Climb again.

Load increments:

| Lift type | Increment |
|---|---|
| Upper body barbell | 1–2.5 kg total |
| Lower body barbell | 2.5–5 kg |
| Dumbbells | smallest pair available |
| Cable stack | one plate |

Do not add weight when only the first set reached the top of the range, or when form degraded to
get there.

## Stall protocol

A stall = three consecutive sessions with no added rep anywhere in the exercise, under the same
conditions.

1. Check rest first. 2–3 min on compounds, 1–2 min on isolation. Short rest is the most common
   cause of a fake stall.
2. Check what precedes it. A new exercise added earlier in the session may be the actual cause.
3. Deload one week: 5–10% off the working weight, about a third fewer sets.
4. Return to the bottom of the rep range and climb again.

Do not rotate exercises every few weeks to "shock the muscle." Changing the movement destroys the
only measurement you have — you can no longer tell progress from unfamiliarity.

## When a weight stack runs out

Home stacks are often 40 kg or less. In order of preference:

1. **Go unilateral** — one-arm row, one-arm pulldown, single-leg curl. Instantly doubles relative
   load and adds a symmetry check for free.
2. **Raise the rep range** — 12–20, up to 20–30 if needed, taken to RIR 1–2. Light loads near
   failure grow muscle comparably to heavy ones; what they don't build as well is maximal strength.
3. **Slow the eccentric and pause at peak contraction.** More stimulus at the same load.
4. **Move the movement to a barbell** where real overload is available, and leave the stack for
   isolation.

Never hang extra plates on a stack unless the manufacturer explicitly permits it.

## What to record

Without a log, none of the above works — double progression needs last session's numbers.

Minimum viable entry: `exercise, weight, reps per set, RIR on the last set`

```
bench 60 kg — 10/9/8, RIR 2
```

That single line supports every decision in this document.
